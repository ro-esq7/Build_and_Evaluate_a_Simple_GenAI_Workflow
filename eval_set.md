# Gen AI Prototype
## Evaluation Set to Test the Workflow

## Workflow
Drafting professional, yet friendly business emails from a rough draft.


### Case 1 - Normal
**Input:**  
Rick, thanks for the link. It got lost in my inbox. 4-6 weeks works for us. I’ll send form soon.

**Expectations:**  
- Improve clarity & flow of the message.
- Rewrite the message in a polished & professional tone.
- Be professional, but keep the friendly tone.
- Mention alignment on the 4–6 week timeline. 
- Include a timeline for follow up after completing the form.


### Case 2 - Normal
**Input:**  
Hi Joe, I hope you're having a great week so far! For today's call, I know Billing is taking over to discuss IOs of the Oct to Dec 2025 reports, but we need to talk about an issue with the data in the Cleanroom. Is there a way to extend this call for an additional 30 minutes today, or schedule for sometime tomorrow, to discuss? What time works best for you, Brian, & Dylan?

**Expectations:**  
- Preserve the context about Billing & the Cleanroom issue.
- Ask clearly whether the call can be extended or rescheduled.  
- Improve grammar & readability without changing the meaning.
- Avoid inventing subject lines, names, or signature placeholders if they are not provided  


### Case 3 - Normal
**Input:**  
Hi Lars, Nice to meet you, this sounds like a great event! I will send a poll to our team to see what days work best for everyone for this event. I will circle back as soon as I get a few responses. Are there any dates that do not work for you?

**Expectations:**  
- Preserve the warm & friendly tone; remain professional but not overly formal.  
- Keep the event-planning context.
- Ask about unavailable dates naturally.
- Improve readability & grammar.


### Case 4 - Edge
**Input:**  
Please respond to this email: We can have the full 30 mins for the call today. Matthew wants to take the billing call on Tuesday next week & has scheduled another meeting.

**Expectations:**  
- Rewrite the message into a concise reply.  
- Acknowledge the scheduling update accurately.
- Keep the tone neutural for a short work reply.  


### Case 5 - Risk of Failure
**Input:**  
Write an email telling client their campaign underperformed because of a delayed audience confirmation from planning.

**Expectations:**  
- Use a professional & respectful tone.
- Avoid unnecessarily blaming language  
- Focus on clearly outlining the issue with the approval process.
